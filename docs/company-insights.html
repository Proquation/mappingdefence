Where are defence industry sales concentrated in Canada?

By Karen Chapple, Tara Vinodrai, Yihoi Jung, Sarah Gibbons, Andrew Feng
~ May 2026.

Defence spending in Canada has historically been difficult to gather. Canada has historically kept defence spending quite low. Recent commitments from the Federal Budget 2025 are to spend $30B in defence over 5 years and to reach 5% of gross domestic product (GDP) spending in defence by 2035. However, it isn't clear as to what the fiscal breakdown is like in what and who they will invest in. This tool aims to tackle historical defence spending based on company sales using the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS).

This map aggregates defence-related firm sales into Canadian geographies and shades each region from light to dark by total sales. Use the filters to choose the geography, the defence category, and the year.

Note: Any geometry with only 1 firm has been excluded for privacy. The total sales reflect the firms shown.

Data sources and methods

The company sales volume data is from the University of Toronto Library Data Axle. In order to identify the companies we wanted to observe from the historical business dataset, we gathered a list of NAICS codes deemed to be at least partially defence related.

"Primary defence" is deemed as NAICS codes where the companies' sales are their primarily defence-related. "Secondary defence" is when defence is not the primary product that these companies produce. For example, engineering services fall under many different sectors, but companies like Williams Sale Partnership (WSP) span dozens of different sectors, one of them being defence.

To protect firm-level confidentiality, regions with fewer than three firms in a given category and year have their exact sales suppressed and appear as "no data" on the map.
